# What Roblox developers say on YouTube about running Roblox Ads (2024-2026)

Method note: YouTube was searched with yt-dlp (`ytsearch`) for "roblox ads manager", "roblox sponsored ads how much I spent", "I spent robux on roblox ads", "roblox game marketing ads 2025", "roblox ads tutorial cost per play", "how my roblox game went viral ads", "roblox ads tycoon game results", "roblox ads simulator ccu revenue", "roblox ads home recommendations 2026", "roblox ad credits strategy", "roblox ads thumbnail ctr test". The youtube-transcript-api package was blocked (RequestBlocked), but yt-dlp could download YouTube's auto-generated English captions (sometimes only the `en-orig` track worked). **All 24 videos in the table below were actually transcribed from auto-captions**, so the timestamps are from the caption files (accurate to about ±20s). Auto-captions mishear some words (e.g. "CCP" for CPP, "CU" for CCU). One older video (Mulch 2021, yG44d_Xnbwg) was found and left out as outdated. Ad credits: several creators say 1 ad credit ≈ $1 USD, and in-video conversions show 1 credit ≈ 263-285 Robux (Poke: 1,000 credits = 263,000 Robux; JustDucksey: 10 credits = 2,850 Robux; Yann: 380 credits = 100K Robux).

**IMPORTANT CONTEXT (May-July 2026):** Roblox launched "Roblox Kids and Select" on about May 19, 2026. A new game is shown only to 16+ age-checked users until it collects enough "highly engaged" players. Every 2026 video after May refers to this as "the new eligibility requirements", and it changes the ad math (see Q1 and Q4). Advice from before May 2026 about very cheap launches ($5/day) may be outdated.

## Budgets, daily spend, campaign length and settings

### Takeaway
There is strong agreement: use a daily budget (not lifetime), the "maximize plays" goal, all regions/devices/audiences, and $5-$20 a day for 5-14 days. A typical first launch is about $50-$200. Since Kids/Select (May 2026), creators say you need to spend more and run longer, with ~$15-20/day for 7-10 days as the new norm.

### Cited Findings
- MoseyTube77's default is "$10 a day for 10 days, which is $100 total", going up to $15-20/day for competitive genres or games that need full servers. His reason for 10 days: "that gives the algorithm enough time to actually evaluate the game" — [MoseyTube77 1:00-1:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d9UxSCiz3us&t=60)
- MoseyTube77's "Guess the Job or Die": $20/day until about $179 total, stopped "because the game started getting players from home recommendations". The game peaked at 3,000 CCU, has 2.1M visits, and earned 358,166 Robux in 72 hours (~$12,535/month DevEx) — [MoseyTube77 1:52-2:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5zz5ao0zV_Y&t=112); [MoseyTube77 0:23-1:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a170E9klbP4&t=23)
- MoseyTube77's "Defend Your Brainrot": $10/day for 10 days, 20K+ visits, then organic home recommendation traffic and ~48,000 Robux (~$177 DevEx) — [MoseyTube77 4:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d9UxSCiz3us&t=277)
- Yann's settings: "daily budget, maximize plays, all regions, all devices", plus as many thumbnails as he can afford — [Yann 2:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yw32DZtz4mQ&t=155)
- Yann's simulator: $8/day × 7 days = $56. Brainrot tag game: $13/day × 14 days = $182. New racing game: $20/day for ~10 days. He says "for most genres ... now ... $15 a day for 7 to 10 days will be solid", and also: "Back then, you could run $5 a day on a simulator and be in the algorithm in 6 days, and that's just likely not the case anymore" — [Yann 4:02-4:46, 8:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yw32DZtz4mQ&t=242)
- Yann's "Hatch a Dragon" (egg-hatching simulator): 380 ad credits (=100K Robux), $20/day for 19 days ("definitely longer than I would have wanted"), 30-day CTR 3.3% (closer to 4.5% after "unlocked all audiences"), cost per play 0.006, ~95K visits, 60K Robux earned back in 28 days on a 100K Robux spend (~$600 total project cost). Home rec impressions began "at least a week" after ads started — [Yann 0:22-2:36, 4:04-4:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bw2ty2T46z4&t=22)
- CallMeDogey: "always do daily budget. Never, ever, ever do lifetime", because "Roblox loves consistency and retention" and wants a steady daily flow of players. He usually sets $5-8/day: "$16 a day is kind of crazy". Run at least 7 days, ideally around 10 ("$5 a day for 10 days ... a pretty decent starting line"). For a game you expect to be popular, budget $50-100 USD in total — [CallMeDogey 6:26-8:02, 4:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7QfaQe4rsq8&t=386)
- CallMeDogey's results across games: about $50 spend leading to 1.7M visits; under $50 leading to 1.1M (troll tower); ~$200 leading to only 40K ("people just didn't like it enough"); $60 leading to 241K; ~$60 leading to 1M+; $30 leading to ~500K. "That $50 worth only gains the game about 10,000, maybe 20,000 visits and then it picked up on the algorithm" — [CallMeDogey 1:53-4:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7QfaQe4rsq8&t=113)
- bubDEV (simulator/tycoon templates): maximize plays, lifetime budget, "no more than 400 ... the sweet spot I go for is around 250" (credits) over about 5 days, at most 7: "your game should hit algorithm within 7 days ... if your game still needs ads after like 14 days ... it might be cooked". The example game reached 3,000 CCU after 5 days of ads, then ads were turned off and home recommendations took over — [bubDEV 2:40-4:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7z8rfg_jBuk&t=160). (He uses lifetime budget where others use daily; this is a disagreement between sources.)
- Tizzy RBLX: "bare minimum, $16 a day ... at least 3 days", ideally a week. Daily budget, all players, Roblox's recommended settings, no advanced targeting. Pay with Robux converted to ad credits ("you're going to save a ton of money") rather than USD — [Tizzy 12:06-15:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=owMnhg3fn_M&t=726)
- Seibo's 1v1 IQ Race (Aug-Sep 2026): 10-16 credits/day at the start, then 14/day, then 8-10/day, then 3/day, cut back as home recommendations grew. ~170 credits on the main campaign, game at ~897 CCU — [Seibo 0:24-3:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FmAD02FS_3k&t=24)
- Raul (first campaign): 16 credits/day for 5 days. ~52 credits total led to 400K impressions, 1,800+ plays, 135 hours of playtime, and only 137 Robux earned — [Raul 2:14, 6:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7vYv8DA7pqU&t=134)
- Poke: 1,000 credits (~263,000 Robux) in one week. Peaked at ~115 CCU and 11K unique players on the best day. "when the ad ended, so did the player count. I lost every player" — [Poke 6:56-7:41, 11:49-13:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VSVCfNHK2IE&t=416)
- Official Roblox (Ads Manager team): all campaigns currently use the "maximize plays" goal. New games should leave audiences open; older games should target "new users"; after an update, target "lapsed users" (played 30-180 days ago). Targeting narrows the audience and raises cost. Up to 10 custom thumbnails per campaign. The learning period is ~24 hours — [Roblox Learn 0:42-3:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5HcH-9E7USc&t=42)
- On timing: bubDEV says start date and time "doesn't really matter" — [bubDEV 4:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7z8rfg_jBuk&t=270). MoseyTube77 and Yann say there is "no hidden trick" in region or time of day — [MoseyTube77 1:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d9UxSCiz3us&t=115). Older advice from med_v3 (Mar 2025) says weekend mornings have the most players but more competition, while weekday nights are cheaper — [med_v3 1:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7ZKZo0enAMU&t=67)
- Targeting disagreements: med_v3 (Mar 2025) recommends targeting 18+ ages and matching devices to the game, and bubDEV mentions US/CA/UK as the biggest spenders. But bubDEV leaves all devices on because restricting them "significantly lowers the amount of people that see my ads" — [med_v3 1:51-2:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7ZKZo0enAMU&t=111); [bubDEV 4:52-5:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7z8rfg_jBuk&t=292). In practice, Yann found most of his simulator's audience came from "lower spending countries", which hurt his conversion rate and ARPPU — [Yann 1:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bw2ty2T46z4&t=111)

### Inferences
- A sensible baseline for an idle/sim game launch in late 2026: daily budget of ~$10-20 (≈10-20 credits), maximize plays, broad targeting, 7-10 days, with $100-$200 set aside, and a plan to extend if home rec impressions are rising but not yet enough.
- If a game draws a mostly low-spending audience, monetization suffers (Yann). Geo-targeting tier-1 countries might help revenue but cost more per play; nobody tested this with data.

### Gaps
- Nobody published a controlled test of geo-targeting or age-targeting under the current Ads Manager.
- The new "engagement" objective (for reaching highly engaged players, launched in 2026) was not covered with results in any video found.

## CTR, cost per play and other benchmarks devs watch

### Takeaway
The most repeated benchmarks are CTR ≥2.5% (1% is bad, ~2% average, 3%+ good, 4-6% on very clickable genres) and cost per play ≤0.008 credits (~$0.008). Analytics lag by 1-3 days, so don't react to the first day.

### Cited Findings
- "You want to be somewhere around 2.5% click-through rate or better, and around $0.008 cost per play or better" — [MoseyTube77 2:41-3:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d9UxSCiz3us&t=161); Yann gives the same numbers and notes a would-you-rather tower might get 8% CTR while "a niche simulator ... only ... 2.5" — [Yann 5:52-6:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yw32DZtz4mQ&t=352)
- "On Roblox, 1% is bad and getting 2% is actually just average." Raul's CTR rose from 1% to 1.95% after redoing thumbnails — [Raul 4:33-4:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7vYv8DA7pqU&t=273)
- CallMeDogey: almost 3% CTR is "absolutely great. It's pretty hard to go above that". His CTR dropped ≥0.8 points in under a week (creative fatigue), so he says to make your own thumbnails so you can refresh them weekly — [CallMeDogey thumbnails 0:23-0:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SjjPGduApZU&t=23)
- Real campaign figures: JustVeryYellow obby: 40 credits, 440K impressions, 5.69% CTR, 24K clicks, 6,738 plays (~0.006 credits/play), 61 hours of playtime, average playtime 2-4 minutes, 22 Robux from ads — [JustVeryYellow 9:58-10:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rzB8CcRzFK8&t=598). Tycxly obby: 19.43 credits gave 7.1K players at 4.73% CTR, peak 40 CCU, 780 Robux — [Tycxly 2:40-4:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3TBrOqW4U1I&t=160). Yann's simulator: CTR 3.3-4.5%, CPP 0.006 — [Yann](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bw2ty2T46z4&t=22)
- Clicks show up before plays: Raul saw 3,000 clicks but only 17 plays at first. "The analytics were just delayed" and the plays appeared the next day — [Raul 5:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7vYv8DA7pqU&t=344). Tizzy: Roblox dashboards update "like every 3 days" — [Tizzy 13:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=owMnhg3fn_M&t=796)
- Benchmarks (the six stats in the game overview) are worse while players come from ads: "If all of your stats are above the 50th percentile while on ads, you're in a good spot" — [Yann 6:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yw32DZtz4mQ&t=398). Older advice (med_v3, Mar 2025): good CTR ~3%, good qualified play-through rate ~2.25% — [med_v3 2:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7ZKZo0enAMU&t=178)
- rasdev: Roblox ranks games on engagement (aim for 10+ minute sessions), retention (D1) and monetization, and on a "rolling 7-day window", so after a fix "you need a full week of better data to overwrite your old bad data" — [rasdev 0:42-1:26, 3:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7VhmLFkhwPU&t=42)
- bubDEV: D7 retention is "literally why the game is doing so well". Average playtime of 12.3 minutes on a template game making ~24-40K Robux a day — [bubDEV slop 5:39-6:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N-u2gI4DUyw&t=339)

### Inferences
- Rough targets for an idle game's ad phase: CTR ≥2.5% (simulators typically 2.5-4.5%), CPP ≤0.008 credits, session time ≥10 minutes, and benchmarks ≥50th percentile while on ads.

### Gaps
- No reliable published CPC figures under the current system (the cost-per-click and bid figures in the 2024-early 2025 videos are from the old auction system).

## Thumbnails/creatives: how many to test and how winners are chosen

### Takeaway
Upload many creatives at once (2-3 at minimum; Tizzy says 10-20) and let the ad system send traffic to the best one. On days 2-4, remove or replace the losers by CTR, then set the winner as the game's main thumbnail. Copy the concepts of proven top-game thumbnails, and fix the title/"core fantasy" together with the thumbnail.

### Cited Findings
- MoseyTube77: "at least two or three different ones and run them all at the same time to see which one gets better clicks" — [MoseyTube77 2:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d9UxSCiz3us&t=138)
- Tizzy: "launch as many as possible, at least 10. If you really want to win, launch ... 20 thumbnails" — [Tizzy 12:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=owMnhg3fn_M&t=726). Official limit: up to 10 custom thumbnails per campaign — [Roblox Learn 2:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5HcH-9E7USc&t=141) (this conflicts with Tizzy's "20", which may mean across campaigns)
- CallMeDogey: put all thumbnails in the ad and "it should direct all the attention to the best performing thumbnail ... that's how you know which one to select on your game" as the main thumbnail for home recommendations. A separate test ad now costs a minimum lifetime budget of $25 — [CallMeDogey 8:46-9:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7QfaQe4rsq8&t=526)
- Yann / MoseyTube77: on days 2-4, swap out thumbnails with poor CTR or remove them and let the better ones run — [Yann 5:30-5:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yw32DZtz4mQ&t=330); [MoseyTube77 3:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d9UxSCiz3us&t=209)
- Raul: you can't swap a thumbnail on an active ad; he "had to completely cancel the old one and lose all the momentum". After rebuilding thumbnails to copy popular games, CTR nearly doubled (1% to 1.9%) and the game got 800+ plays — [Raul 3:37-4:10, 6:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7vYv8DA7pqU&t=217)
- Tizzy case study: CTR went from 1.54% to 3.27% after renaming "Sell Your Data" to "Hack a Business" and making new thumbnails. Method: keyword research in Creator Exchange (look for keywords where several games have big visit counts), write titles at a 3rd-grade reading level with a clear "core fantasy" and an implied challenge (e.g. verb + noun like "Grow a Garden"), and make thumbnails by combining a proven thumbnail layout with the keyword's visual theme. "The title gives context to the thumbnail" — [Tizzy 0:00-11:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=owMnhg3fn_M)
- CallMeDogey: "reference images are your savior". Recreate a top game's thumbnail layout at 1920×1080 in Photopea, or use AI tools with a reference image — [CallMeDogey thumbnails 1:07-1:28, 5:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SjjPGduApZU&t=67)
- bubDEV: thumbnails must be readable in a split second: "you should be able to understand what the game is by just literally looking at the thumbnail" — [bubDEV 4:30-4:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7z8rfg_jBuk&t=270)
- Mulch: changing the thumbnail to a trending meme (Squid Game) made it "the best performing one". Trend thumbnails go stale within about a month (chicken jockey, then Tung Tung Sahur, then Squid Game) — [Mulch 7:11-8:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B2vC4-QArWg&t=431)
- rasdev: an odd, curiosity-driven icon "nine times out of 10 beats a flashy ... generic one" — [rasdev 2:31-2:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7VhmLFkhwPU&t=151)

### Inferences
- For an idle country/strategy game, the title should state a clear fantasy in simple words (e.g. "Build an Empire", "Rule a Country"), with 5-10 thumbnail variants based on top idle/tycoon thumbnails. Expect to refresh creatives every week or two as CTR decays.

### Gaps
- Nobody quantified how many impressions are needed before calling a winner. Advice is just "after the day-1 learning period, on days 2-4".

## When to start ads and when to stop (ads vs organic recommendations)

### Takeaway
Nearly every creator treats ads as a way to give the algorithm data, not as the growth engine. Fix bugs, the tutorial and monetization first. Run ads until home recommendation traffic is large enough to carry the game (Tizzy: when home rec impressions are greater than or equal to ad impressions; Seibo: taper as home recs cross ad plays; Yann: when the lowest-CCU hour still fills two lobbies). Stop early if benchmarks stay bad by day 5. Without good retention, players disappear as soon as ads stop.

### Cited Findings
- "Roblox has no idea if your game is good yet ... ads are basically just how you give Roblox enough data to make that decision ... if those numbers actually look good, Roblox will start to push your game on its own through ... home recommendations" — [MoseyTube77 0:23-0:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d9UxSCiz3us&t=23); bubDEV says the same: "your game can't be pushed by the Roblox algorithm if it's not in the algorithm to begin with" — [bubDEV 1:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7z8rfg_jBuk&t=68)
- MoseyTube77's timeline: day 1, don't touch anything (learning) and watch CTR/CPP plus real players. Days 2-4, swap thumbnails and fix bugs. Day 5+, "be honest with yourself": stop and fix the core if stats are rough, or keep going if home rec impressions are appearing, and stop once organic traffic sustains the game — [MoseyTube77 2:41-4:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d9UxSCiz3us&t=161)
- Yann's version adds: day 1, watch the error log, the funnel and live players. Halve game pass and product prices during week 1 to get a realistic payer conversion rate and ARPPU. Low D7 retention is "a core loop issue", and daily rewards won't fix it. Stop once the lowest-CCU hour of the day still has "at least two full lobbies". Ads "should never last more than 2 weeks" — [Yann 4:46-8:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yw32DZtz4mQ&t=286)
- Tizzy: "Once your home recommendation impressions have surpassed your ad impressions, stop ads" (e.g. ~200K sponsored impressions per week means wait for ~200K home rec impressions). "Usually it takes at least a week of running ads for you to start getting your first home impressions." Push updates the same day stats arrive — [Tizzy 14:24-16:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=owMnhg3fn_M&t=864)
- Seibo cut daily credits once home recommendation plays crossed sponsored plays — [Seibo 0:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FmAD02FS_3k&t=47)
- rasdev: "Ads and recommendations are two completely separate systems ... If your game is not optimized, running ads is literally paying money to prove to Roblox that you don't have a game that is ready yet" — [rasdev 3:16-3:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7VhmLFkhwPU&t=196)
- Things that went wrong: Poke (115 CCU fell to 0 when ads ended, with a 29% like ratio and many bugs) — [Poke 9:42-12:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VSVCfNHK2IE&t=582). DitchyDevelopments' 24-hour game peaked at ~20 players and dropped within an hour — [Ditchy 0:21-1:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cfLbW2j2VLs&t=21). JustVeryYellow's game "dried out like two days after the ads ended" — [JustVeryYellow 6:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rzB8CcRzFK8&t=382)
- Raul delayed ads for about a week of daily bug-fix updates and added monetization (copied game passes turned into cheaper temporary boosts) before launching — [Raul 0:45-2:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7vYv8DA7pqU&t=45)
- Mulch spent 100K Robux over about 2 months while the game was buggy and lightly monetized: ~107K visits and 1,900 favorites, but 731 Robux total. "I guess it needs to be more in your face" — [Mulch 1:53-2:39, 9:04-9:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B2vC4-QArWg&t=113)
- bubDEV: a few thousand Roblox search impressions were enough to get a template game into the algorithm without ads, and ranking high in search against bigger games is "a very good indicator" — [bubDEV 7:33-8:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7z8rfg_jBuk&t=453)
- **Kids/Select (2026):** new games are shown only to 16+ age-checked users until they reach a highly engaged player threshold. The DevForum announcement says the initial requirement is 500 highly engaged players, with maintenance lowered to 25 over 60 days as of July 16, 2026. There is also an optional 100,000 Robux expedited review (refundable after 90 days if eligibility is kept) and a new Ads Manager "engagement" objective aimed at users who count toward the threshold — [DevForum announcement](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/roblox-kids-and-select-global-launch-upcoming-updates-to-eligibility-ads-manager-and-expedited-review/4685717). The current creator docs say **250 unique plays** from highly engaged players within 60 days, where highly engaged means account age, play history, and a purchase on Roblox in the last 60 days — [Roblox Creator Docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/kids-and-select). (500 vs 250 conflict: the threshold appears to have been lowered/relaxed since launch; trust the docs.)
- Creators on Kids/Select: Tizzy says don't use age targeting; "all ages" gets served to 16+ anyway, and don't panic over slow qualification — [Tizzy 12:53-13:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=owMnhg3fn_M&t=773). Yann says in hindsight he "would have paid for the highly engaged players 100,000 Robux fee", since his game picked up home recs only after qualifying around day 13 — [Yann 2:36, 4:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bw2ty2T46z4&t=156). DitchyDevelopments: "you need a minimum of 500 active monthly players to get published beyond 16+" — [Ditchy description](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cfLbW2j2VLs)

### Inferences
- Under Kids/Select, the first ad goal is to qualify for under-16 reach. That favors players who already spend on Roblox (tier-1 regions, older accounts), and budgets should cover the 1-2 extra weeks it may take. Paying the refundable 100K Robux expedited review could save ad money if the game keeps its eligibility.
- For an idle game, retention (D1/D7) and session length decide whether ad-driven players turn into home recommendations. Ads cannot fix a weak loop.

### Gaps
- No creator has published a before/after comparison of ad efficiency under Kids/Select vs before it.

## Common mistakes

### Takeaway
The repeated mistakes: launching ads on a buggy, under-monetized game; running weak thumbnails or titles; overreacting on day 1 or to lagging analytics; stopping ads before home recs arrive; dumping a big budget in one day; and expecting ads to make the game go viral.

### Cited Findings
- Running ads before the game is ready, or with too little monetization to earn anything back (Raul, Mulch, Poke, JustVeryYellow) — [Raul](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7vYv8DA7pqU&t=45); [Mulch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B2vC4-QArWg&t=113)
- Keeping old thumbnails out of laziness: "I lost money because I was too lazy to make a few changes" — [Raul 1:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7vYv8DA7pqU&t=67)
- Spending the whole budget in one day: Mulch's audience called his earlier 100K-in-a-day campaign "stupid", so he spread the next one over days — [Mulch 0:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B2vC4-QArWg&t=22)
- Panicking during the learning period or over delayed plays (Raul nearly abandoned his game; the official guidance is to check back after ~24 hours) — [Raul 5:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7vYv8DA7pqU&t=344); [Roblox Learn 2:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5HcH-9E7USc&t=176)
- Believing in "secret settings": "These are not Facebook ads. Do not treat them like that. I've actually gotten the best results using the default settings" — [bubDEV 6:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7z8rfg_jBuk&t=363)
- Running more ads on a dying game instead of fixing its worst metric — [rasdev 3:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7VhmLFkhwPU&t=196)
- Choosing a niche genre (realistic FPS, racing) means a much higher cost to reach an audience — [CallMeDogey 1:31-1:53, 5:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7QfaQe4rsq8&t=91); [Yann 3:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yw32DZtz4mQ&t=220)
- Leaving the "auto reload" ad credit option on can spend extra Robux — [LovJak 4:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=feVPsViqceo&t=256)

### Inferences
- The best return comes from having monetization (starter pack, 2x cash, spins/luck products) and a tutorial ready before day 1 of ads, so ad players produce real ARPPU and conversion data.

### Gaps
- No quantified data on how much of a campaign's spend is wasted by restarting campaigns (lost "momentum").

## Advice specific to idle, simulator, tycoon or strategy games

### Takeaway
Simulators and tycoons are seen as the cheapest genre to advertise because the audience is huge and they work solo (no full lobbies needed). $5-15/day for about a week used to work, but costs are higher after Kids/Select. Idle/sim campaigns that succeeded still depended on D1/D7 retention and layered monetization. Low-spending regions and low-end devices hurt sims with heavy visuals.

### Cited Findings
- "If your game is a simulator or something you could play alone, you can spend less ... There's a million simulators out there, but there's also a billion people that play them. So, you don't need to spend as much money getting a simulator into the algorithm" — [Yann 3:19-3:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yw32DZtz4mQ&t=199)
- bubDEV: "any of you guys making simulator or tycoon types of games similar to like grow a garden ... this [Roblox sponsored ads] is probably the method that you're going to want to use". His example sim reached 3,000 CCU from about 5 days of ads at ~250 credits — [bubDEV 2:40-3:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7z8rfg_jBuk&t=160)
- Yann's egg-hatching idle simulator (Hatch a Dragon): 100K Robux on ads earned 60K Robux back in 28 days. D1 retention fell from 13% to ~5-8%. Top earners were the "10x spins" dev product (admin egg on a wheel), an exclusive dragon, and rebirth skips; game passes (2x cash etc.) earned less. Problems: a copycat idea not original enough, FPS issues on low-end devices, and mostly low-spending regions. Reaching highly engaged status around day 13 coincided with home rec growth — [Yann 0:00-7:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bw2ty2T46z4)
- Seibo's 1v1 IQ Race reached ~900 CCU on tapered spend (14 to 10 to 3 credits/day) — [Seibo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FmAD02FS_3k&t=83)
- LovJak's Grow-a-Garden clone: 10 credits/day for 3 days gave a peak of 10 CCU, bad 7-day stats and 656 Robux; "probably not" going organic — [LovJak 3:51-7:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=feVPsViqceo&t=231)
- FliedaDev's idle "Roll A Grass" (roll, place grass, earn, upgrade): 6 credits/day, only 2-7 players overnight after 4 credits spent (no final result shown) — [FliedaDev 0:21-1:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9AjRPKdinVw&t=21)
- Monetization layering that worked for a 3K-CCU game: daily, playtime, group and VIP rewards; a streak with paid streak recovery; collectibles with a rotating shop; a clippable steal mechanic; VIP 1.5x and 2x passes; hint products — [MoseyTube77 1:56-4:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a170E9klbP4&t=116). A limited-time purchasable item added on update day boosted a template game's ARPPU — [bubDEV slop 6:49-7:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N-u2gI4DUyw&t=409)
- Strategy/Clash-style games: no video found.

### Inferences
- For an idle country/bunker game, expect sim-like ad costs (CTR target ~2.5-4%, CPP ~0.006-0.008 credits). Plan ~$15-20/day for 7-14 days to get through the Kids/Select gate, have monetization tuned before ads (Yann's spins/luck products earned the most), and keep the game light on low-end mobile devices.

### Gaps
- No creator video covered strategy, base-building or attack/defense idle games specifically. Advice above is drawn from simulators and tycoons.
